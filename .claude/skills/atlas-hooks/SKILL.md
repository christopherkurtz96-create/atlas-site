# Atlas Excavation & Demolition Hook Writing Skill — Research-Backed
## Applies To: Facebook (Primary) · Instagram · GBP · LinkedIn · Video
## Brand: Atlas Excavation & Demolition — Demo, Excavation, Site Prep in Mid-Missouri

---

## Brand Context

**Atlas Excavation & Demolition** handles structural demolition, excavation, and site prep for property owners and general contractors in the Columbia, MO area (45-60 minute radius).

**Primary avatars:**
1. Property owners (residential) — First-time demo buyers, 35-65, nervous about cost/process
2. Property owners (commercial) — Investors/developers, care about timeline and permits
3. General contractors — Need reliable subs who show up and communicate
4. Realtors / Property managers — Need a contractor they can confidently refer

**Voice:** Blue-collar professional. Direct. Honest. No-BS. Confident but not cocky. Sounds like a contractor who also knows how to run a business.

**Voice test:** Would a Columbia, MO homeowner with an old shed to tear down trust this? Would a GC running 5 builds send this to his project manager?

---

## What the Research Says

**Attention window by platform:**
- Mobile: 1.7 seconds before scroll decision
- Instagram Reels: First 2 seconds critical
- Video: 65% who pass 3 seconds watch to 10 seconds
- Facebook: ~300 characters / 3 lines before truncation — that IS the hook
- LinkedIn: "See more" fold at ~210 characters (~35 words)

**For a local service business, hooks need to:**
- Show real work (visual proof > claims)
- Reference the local area (Mid-Missouri, Columbia, specific cities)
- Speak directly to a specific avatar's problem
- Be immediately understandable — no industry jargon the customer doesn't know

## The Eight Hook Archetypes — Atlas Calibration

---

### Archetype 1: The Visual Transformation
The most powerful hook for demo/excavation. Before → After. The image IS the hook.

**Formula:** [Dramatic visual] + [brief context of what happened]

**Atlas examples:**
```
This house sat abandoned for 6 years.
72 hours later — clean lot, graded flat, ready to build.
[PHOTO: Before/After side by side]

An 8,000 sq ft commercial building in Jefferson City.
Gone in 4 days. Site graded and clean.
[PHOTO: Before/After]

What 3 days of work looks like.
[CAROUSEL: Day 1 → Day 2 → Day 3 → Clean site]
```

**Best on:** Facebook, Instagram (carousel and Reels), GBP posts. This is Atlas's bread and butter.

---

### Archetype 2: The Curiosity Gap
Signals valuable information without revealing it. Works for education content.

**Atlas examples:**
```
Most people call 3-5 contractors for a demo job.
Here's why that process costs you more than the demolition itself.

There's one thing that doubles the cost of a demolition job.
Most property owners don't find out until the estimate.

The biggest mistake property owners make before tearing down a structure
has nothing to do with picking the wrong contractor.
```

**Best on:** Facebook text posts, LinkedIn, blog promotion posts.

---

### Archetype 3: The Specificity Hook
Real numbers, real projects, real timeframes. Instant credibility.

**Atlas examples:**
```
2,400 sq ft house. Built in 1962. Asbestos siding.
Abatement coordinated. Structure down. Site graded.
5 days start to finish. Columbia, MO.

A property owner called on Monday.
Site visit Tuesday. Estimate Wednesday.
Crew on-site the following Monday. Shed gone by lunch.

14,000 sq ft commercial demo in Jefferson City.
6-day timeline. Zero delays. Here's how.
```

**Best on:** Facebook, Instagram captions, GBP posts, LinkedIn.

### Archetype 6: The Education Hook
Leads with something the audience doesn't know. Positions Atlas as the expert.

**Atlas examples:**
```
Here's what actually drives the cost of a demolition job
(it's not the size of the building):

Most people don't know: before you tear down anything in Missouri,
you need to check for asbestos. Here's what that process looks like.

"Can I just knock it down myself?"
You can. Here's why that usually costs more in the long run.
```

**Best on:** Facebook, blog posts, GBP posts, LinkedIn. Education content builds trust with first-time buyers.

**Best on:** Instagram Reels (show the clean site first, then the demo), Facebook, GBP.

## Platform-Specific Hook Rules

### Facebook (Primary)
- Fold: ~300 characters / 3 lines. Most Atlas audience is here.
- Photos of real work outperform text-only posts 3:1
- Story hooks and direct address perform strongest
- Warm, direct tone. Write like you're talking to a neighbor.

### Instagram
- Reels: First frame = most dramatic moment (machine hitting structure, debris falling, clean site reveal)
- Carousel Slide 1: 6-10 words max. Show the transformation or state the deliverable.
- Caption: 125 characters before fold — hook must live entirely there

### Google Business Profile
- GBP posts are short: 100-300 words total. Hook is the first sentence.
- Always include a photo of the actual project
- Include city name in every post ("commercial demo in Jefferson City")
- CTA button: "Call Now" or "Get Quote"

### LinkedIn (GC-Focused)
- Professional tone but still direct and honest
- Focus on reliability, timeline, and communication — what GCs care about
- Hook under 35 words before "see more"

### Video (All Platforms)
- Open with the most dramatic visual moment — never a logo or introduction
- On-screen text within first 1-2 seconds carrying the hook
- Never start with: your name, "hey guys," company name, or any setup

## Hook Audit — Run Every Hook Through This

1. **Is there a visual?** For Atlas, if there's no photo or video of real work, the hook is fighting uphill.
2. **Is it specific?** Can vague language be replaced with square footage, days, city name?
3. **Does it target one avatar?** Property owner ≠ GC ≠ realtor. Pick one per post.
4. **Does it reference the service area?** Columbia, Mid-Missouri, or a specific city?
5. **Is it in Atlas voice?** Blue-collar professional. Not corporate, not salesy, not trying too hard.
6. **Would someone screenshot this and send it to a friend?** That's the bar.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Fix |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Stock photos or generic graphics | Kills trust instantly for a local service business | Real work photos ONLY |
| No location reference | Misses local SEO signal and doesn't feel personal | Name the city or area in every post |
| Talking to everyone | Property owners and GCs have different problems | Pick one avatar per post |
| Corporate/polished language | Doesn't match how contractors or property owners talk | Write like you're explaining it on a job site |
| Bragging without proof | "We're the best" means nothing | Show the work. Before/after. Numbers. Stories. |
| Engagement bait | "Tag someone who needs this!!!" | Let the content earn the engagement |
| No CTA | Awareness is great but calls pay the bills | Every conversion post needs a phone number or clear next step |
