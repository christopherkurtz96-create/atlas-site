# Atlas Excavation & Demolition Video Script Skill — Research-Backed
## Applies To: Facebook Reels · Instagram Reels · GBP Video · LinkedIn · X
## Brand: Atlas Excavation & Demolition — Demo, Excavation, Site Prep in Mid-Missouri

---

## Brand Context

**Atlas Excavation & Demolition** handles structural demolition, excavation, and site prep in the Columbia, MO area.

**On-camera energy:** Confident, matter-of-fact, blue-collar professional. Not performative. Sounds like a contractor explaining something to a neighbor — knowledgeable, direct, a little proud of the work.

**Pacing:** Natural. Not rushed, not slow. Let the machinery and the work do the talking when possible.

**Physical presence:** Can be on-site or at camera. Hard hat and work gear builds credibility. Job sites are the best backdrop.

**Sample tone sentences:**
- "Here's what 3 days of demo looks like."
- "Most people don't know this about demolition costs."
- "This is what we mean by 'clean site.'"

## What the Research Says

- Videos under 60 seconds: 53% higher engagement
- 65% who pass 3 seconds watch to 10 seconds
- 79% of LinkedIn videos watched sound-off; 50% of IG Reels sound-off
- Captions improve watch time by 12% and retention by 40%
- For service businesses: process videos and transformation videos get highest save/share rates

**For demolition/excavation specifically:**
- Before/after reveals are the highest-performing format
- Equipment in action = scroll-stopping
- The dramatic moment (first hit, structure collapse, clean site reveal) is the hook

## The Three Video Formats

### Format 1: The Job Timelapse / Transformation (15-45 seconds)
**Purpose:** Visual proof. The work speaks for itself. Highest shareability for Atlas.
**Best platforms:** Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels, GBP

**Structure:**
```
Hook shot (0:00-0:03): The dramatic moment or the "before" shot
The work (0:03-0:30): Timelapse or highlight reel of the demo/excavation
The reveal (0:30-0:40): Clean site, graded lot, job done
Text overlay + CTA (0:40-0:45): One line + phone number or "link in bio"
```

**Atlas Timelapse example:**
```
[VISUAL: Abandoned house, overgrown yard, boarded windows]
[ON SCREEN: "6 years abandoned. 3 days to fix."]

[VISUAL: Timelapse — excavator approaching, first wall coming down, debris loading, hauling, grading]
[ON SCREEN: "Atlas Excavation & Demolition — Columbia, MO"]

[VISUAL: Clean, flat, graded lot. Sun shining.]
[ON SCREEN: "Ready to build. | Call (573) XXX-XXXX"]
```

**Shooting tips:**
- Set up a fixed camera angle for timelapses (phone on tripod works)
- Capture the first swing/contact moment — that's the hook frame
- Film the clean site from the same angle as the "before" — same framing amplifies the transformation
- Get drone footage when possible for larger sites

### Format 2: The Quick Education (30-60 seconds)
**Purpose:** Answer one common question. Build trust. Position as expert.
**Best platforms:** Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn

**Structure:**
```
Hook (0:00-0:03): Question or surprising fact
Answer (0:03-0:40): Clear, direct explanation with visual support
Example (0:40-0:50): Reference a real Atlas project
CTA (0:50-0:60): Phone number or next step
```

**Atlas Education example:**
```
[VISUAL: Chris on a job site, hard hat, talking to camera]
[ON SCREEN: "How much does demolition cost?"]

SPOKEN: "The number one question I get: how much does it cost to tear down a house?"

[ON SCREEN: "3 factors"]
SPOKEN: "Honestly, it depends on three things."

[ON SCREEN: "1. Size + material"]
SPOKEN: "First — size and what it's built with. A wood frame house is easier than a concrete block building."

[ON SCREEN: "2. Asbestos"]
SPOKEN: "Second — hazardous materials. If the building has asbestos siding or lead paint, that needs to be abated before we touch it. That adds cost but it's not optional."

[ON SCREEN: "3. Access + disposal"]
SPOKEN: "Third — how easy it is to get equipment in and what it costs to haul and dispose of the debris."

[ON SCREEN: "$8K-$20K typical residential"]
SPOKEN: "For a typical residential house demo in Mid-Missouri, you're usually looking at somewhere between eight and twenty thousand — that's everything included. Demo, hauling, and cleanup."

[ON SCREEN: "Free estimate → (573) XXX-XXXX"]
SPOKEN: "Best way to know for sure? Call us. Site visit's free and we'll have a detailed estimate to you within 72 hours."
```

**Topics for education videos:**
- How much does demolition cost?
- Do you need a permit to tear down a building in Missouri?
- What happens if there's asbestos?
- What's the difference between demo and deconstruction?
- How long does demolition take?
- What does site prep include?
- Rough grade vs. finish grade
- How to prepare your property before demo day
- What to look for in a demolition contractor

## The Hook (0:00-0:03) — Atlas Video-Specific

**The single best hook for Atlas video:** The equipment making first contact with the structure. The moment of impact. This is inherently scroll-stopping.

**Other proven Atlas openings:**
1. **The before shot:** Abandoned, dilapidated structure. Hold for 2 seconds. Then cut to work.
2. **The reveal:** Start with the clean site. "This used to be a [structure]." Then show the process in reverse.
3. **The question:** "How much does it cost to tear down a house?" — direct to camera.
4. **The customer moment:** "She'd been trying to find someone for 4 months."

**NEVER open with:**
- Atlas logo animation
- "Hey guys" / "Welcome back" / "What's up"
- Chris introducing himself before establishing value
- A long panning shot with no action
- Music only with no text or voice for more than 3 seconds

## On-Screen Text Rules

Every key point needs on-screen text. 80% of social video is watched without sound.

- Max 10 words per text frame
- Use bold, high-contrast text (white with black outline works on most footage)
- Text appears within first 1-2 seconds
- Numbers are powerful on screen: "3 days," "$12K," "2,400 sq ft"
- City/location on screen reinforces local SEO even in video

## What to Film on Every Job

Build a content library by capturing these on every project:
1. **Before:** Wide shot of the structure/site before any work. Multiple angles.
2. **First contact:** The moment the excavator touches the structure.
3. **Work in progress:** 15-30 second clips of active demo/excavation.
4. **The dramatic moment:** Walls falling, structure collapsing, big scoop of dirt.
5. **Equipment:** Close-ups of machines working. People love heavy equipment content.
6. **Crew:** Shots of the team working. Humanizes the brand.
7. **After:** Clean site from the same angle as the "before" shot.
8. **Drone (when possible):** Aerial before and after. Dramatic perspective.

**Filming tips:**
- Phone is fine. Horizontal for YouTube/Facebook, vertical for Reels/Stories.
- Steady shots. Use a tripod or lean against something.
- Timelapses: Set up phone on tripod, hit record, let it run. Speed up 8-16x in editing.
- Audio: If talking to camera, minimize background noise or add captions.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| No footage from the actual job | Film something on EVERY project — even 30 seconds |
| Opening with a logo or intro | Start with the work |
| No on-screen text | Add text — most watch without sound |
| Too long with no payoff | Cut ruthlessly. Show the transformation. |
| Shaky, unfocused footage | Use a tripod. Steady even 5-second clips are gold. |
| Only showing finished work | The process is the content — show the demo happening |
| No CTA | Every video needs one direction: call, follow, or visit |
| Polished/corporate feel | Raw, real footage from the job site performs better than produced content |
