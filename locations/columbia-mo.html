Demolition in Columbia

Columbia has a wide spread of building stock, and we see all of it. Around the University of Missouri and the older neighborhoods east and north of downtown, the houses date to the early 1900s through the 1940s: balloon-framed, plaster and lath, limestone or brick foundations, and usually a detached garage that has leaned a little further every year. South and west of downtown, the ranch houses from the 1950s through the 1970s come down cleaner but almost always carry asbestos in the floor tile, siding or duct wrap. Newer subdivisions send us garages, decks, pools and the occasional fire-damaged house.

Typical Columbia demolition work:

  • Whole-house and garage teardowns, including foundation removal and backfill so the lot is ready for the builder
  • Mobile home removal on rural Boone County parcels and in the parks along the edges of town
  • Interior and selective demolition for downtown remodels, rental turnovers near campus and light commercial strip-outs
  • Pool removal, deck and shed removal, and partial teardowns where one wing of a house comes off and the rest stays

A single-story house of 1,200 to 1,600 square feet is usually a two-day job for us: one day to bring it down and load, one day to pull the foundation, backfill and rough grade. Most Columbia teardowns land between 60 and 120 tons of debris depending on foundation type and how much concrete is on the lot. The full menu is on the demolition page.

Concrete and parking lots in Columbia

Columbia has a lot of tired concrete. Strip-center parking lots from the 1980s along Business Loop 70 and Providence, church and school lots that were built for half the traffic they carry now, and thousands of residential driveways poured 4 inches thick on clay that has moved every winter since.

We handle parking lot removal for commercial owners, property managers and churches: break it out, haul it, regrade the base and hand it to the paver or our concrete crew for a new pour. Asphalt lots are the same process with different disposal. For homeowners, driveway replacement is a two-part job: we remove the old slab and prep the base, then our concrete crew pours the new drive under our contract and warranty. Patios, sidewalks, garage slabs, retaining walls and old foundations fall under general concrete removal.

We do not do concrete leveling or patch repair. If the slab is cracked and heaving, it comes out and gets replaced. A 20-by-40 residential driveway at 4 inches thick is roughly 20 tons of concrete; a half-acre commercial lot at 6 inches is closer to 900 tons, which is why lot removal is priced on a site visit and not a phone call.

Permits and disconnects in Boone County

Inside Columbia city limits, the City of Columbia Building and Site Development office issues demolition permits. Outside the city, in Hallsville, Harrisburg, Rocheport, the unincorporated county or anywhere else in Boone County without its own building department, Boone County Resource Management handles it. Both will want proof of utility disconnects before they sign off.

Statewide, the Missouri Department of Natural Resources requires an asbestos inspection before most structures are demolished. If the inspection finds regulated material, there is a 10-working-day notification window before the teardown can start, and a licensed abatement contractor removes the material first. We coordinate the inspection, the abatement and the disconnects (electric, gas, water and sewer cap), but we do not perform abatement ourselves.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you charge extra to come to Columbia?

No. Columbia is where the equipment lives, so mobilization costs us the least here and that shows in the bid. The fixed price covers getting the excavator and trucks to your lot. The only thing that moves with distance is how a small job prices per square foot, and inside Boone County that effect is close to zero.

Who issues demolition permits in Columbia?

Inside city limits, the City of Columbia Building and Site Development office. Outside the city, Boone County Resource Management. Either way, the permit application needs proof that electric, gas, water and sewer have been disconnected or capped, and for most structures a Missouri DNR asbestos inspection report. We handle the coordination as part of the job.

How long does a house teardown take in Columbia?

Plan on two to three working days for a typical single-story house once permits and disconnects are done. Day one is the teardown and most of the loading. Day two is the foundation, backfill and rough grading. Larger two-story houses, full basements or heavy concrete on the lot can push it to four or five days. The permitting and asbestos steps before that usually take two to four weeks.

Can you remove a parking lot in Columbia without shutting the business down?

Usually, yes. We phase commercial lot removal so part of the lot stays open while the other section is broken out and hauled. It adds a day or two but keeps customers and tenants moving. Night and weekend work is possible for lots where daytime closure is not an option.

Do you handle the asbestos inspection for Columbia teardowns?

We coordinate it. Missouri DNR requires an inspection before most demolitions, and if regulated material turns up, a licensed abatement contractor removes it before we start. We line up the inspector and the abatement crew and build the 10-working-day notification into the schedule. We do not perform abatement ourselves.
