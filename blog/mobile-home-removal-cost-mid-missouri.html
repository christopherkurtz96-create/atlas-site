Mobile Home Removal Cost in Mid-Missouri: 2026 Pricing Guide

2026 pricing guide for single-wide and double-wide trailer demolition in Columbia, Boone County and Mid-Missouri.

Mobile Home Removal Cost: The Numbers

Two things set the base price: how wide the unit is and how much is bolted onto it. Everything below is a typical range for Columbia and the surrounding counties.

Line item Typical range (Mid-Missouri)
Single-wide (14 to 18 ft wide), demolition and haul-off $4,000 to $7,000
Double-wide (24 to 32 ft wide), demolition and haul-off $6,000 to $10,000
Add-ons: decks, porches, built-on rooms, carports $500 to $2,000 each
Septic tank pump and cap $400 to $900
Contents left inside $500 to $1,500
Disposal (pass-through at actual loads and tonnage) $40 to $90 per ton
Steel frame scrap Credited back on the invoice

Here's how the bid is built. Mobilization, the excavator, the trucks and the crew are a fixed price. Disposal is a pass-through. You pay what the landfill charges us, at actual loads and tonnage, with the scale tickets attached. We don't bury it in a lump sum.

The steel chassis goes the other way. Every unit sits on an I-beam frame, and the scrap yard pays for it. We credit it back on the invoice at what the yard paid that week, typically a few hundred dollars.

Demolish It or Haul It Away Intact?

This is the first question on most calls, so here's how mobile home removal cost compares with a move.

Demolish it: $4,000 to $10,000 depending on width and add-ons, done in one to two days, unit gone, pad clear. Condition barely matters.

Move it intact: a licensed mobile home transporter typically charges $3,000 to $8,000 to pull the skirting, set the unit on its axles and haul it a short distance. Blocking, leveling, tie-downs and hookups on the new lot run another $2,000 to $5,000. We don't move homes. A transporter does.

My rule of thumb: if the unit is newer than about 2000, in good shape, and somebody has a lot ready for it, moving can pencil out. Otherwise demolition is cheaper.

Trailer Removal Cost by Situation

Width sets the base. The situation sets the rest of your mobile home removal cost. These are the four we see most.

Abandoned unit on a rental lot

The tenant left and the unit is full of belongings and trash. Budget the base price plus $500 to $1,500 for contents, because a full single-wide adds 3 to 5 tons at the same per-ton rate. Finish Missouri's abandoned-home notice process before you call.

Inherited unit

A parent's or grandparent's trailer on family land, often with a deck, a built-on room and a shed. These usually price at $5,500 to $9,000 once the add-ons are counted, and the older ones need an asbestos inspection first. If the estate is still open, the executor signs the contract.

Replacing it with a new manufactured home

The most common job we do. The dealer needs the old unit gone and the pad ready before the new one is delivered. We tear down, haul off, pull the old piers and grade the pad. Removal plus pad prep typically runs $5,000 to $9,000.

Fire or flood damage

Fire jobs run slower because the adjuster usually wants photos before anything moves. Flood-damaged units are heavier, since wet drywall, insulation and carpet run 30 to 50 percent more tonnage than dry material. Expect the top of the range. If the loss is insured, see our posts on whether insurance covers demolition and fire damage demolition.

What Drives the Price

Six things move mobile home removal cost up or down inside those ranges.

  • Size. A single-wide is one steel frame and one pass with the excavator. A double-wide is two frames, twice the roof, twice the floor and twice the trucks. Width is the biggest single factor.
  • Add-ons. Decks, porches, carports and built-on rooms are separate structures with their own footings. Each one is a line item.
  • Contents. Empty is cheaper. Furniture and belongings add time and tonnage.
  • Access. We need an excavator and a container within reach of the unit. A gravel drive to the door is ideal. A narrow lane or soft ground means smaller equipment and a higher number.
  • Distance to the landfill. A site 10 minutes from the transfer station and a site 45 minutes out in Moniteau County have different hauling costs on the same trailer.
  • Asbestos in pre-1980 units. Older trailers often have it in floor tile, ceiling texture or siding. Missouri DNR requires an asbestos inspection first. If regulated material turns up, a Missouri-registered contractor handles asbestos abatement before demolition under our contract. We coordinate it. We don't perform it. Figure $300 to $600 for the inspection and $1,500 to $4,000 for abatement if it's needed. Most 1990s and newer units come back clean.

"Free Mobile Home Removal" and Lowball Quotes

People searching mobile home removal cost run into two things that sound better than they are.

"Free removal" offers. These are real only when somebody wants the home. That means a newer unit, structurally sound, that a buyer or a transporter can pull off on its own axles and resell. Nobody hauls off a 1970s trailer with a soft floor for free.

The one-number quote. A low lump sum usually leaves something out. Ask these before you compare bids:

  • Is disposal included, and will I see the scale tickets?
  • Do the piers, runners, tie-downs and skirting come out, or just the box?
  • Who gets the steel frame scrap?
  • Is the pad graded when you leave?
  • Do I get a signed letter for the title saying the home was destroyed?

Permits and Utilities: Columbia vs. Boone County

Inside Columbia city limits: City of Columbia Building and Site Development issues the demolition permit. Plan on a few days to a week once the application and the utility disconnect confirmations are in.

Unincorporated Boone County: there is no county demolition permit. You still need documented utility disconnects and the asbestos inspection on older units.

Step-by-step details are in our guide to demolition permits in Columbia.

Utility disconnects come before any of it: electric, gas or propane, water, and the sewer or septic line. We coordinate the calls, but the accounts are in your name, so the requests come from you. Allow one to three weeks. Our post on utility disconnects before demolition covers who to call.

Title and Paperwork

In Missouri a mobile home has a title, like a vehicle, unless the owner filed an affidavit of affixation that made it part of the real estate. We need to know which one you have before we tear it down.

  • You have the title: after demolition, surrender it to the Missouri Department of Revenue and tell the county assessor so it comes off the personal property roll. Our demolition letter is your proof.
  • Inherited: the estate transfers the title or the executor signs.
  • Abandoned on your lot: finish the state notice process, then we schedule.
  • Lien on the title: the lienholder has to release it. We won't demolish a unit with an open lien.

None of this changes your mobile home removal cost, but it's the thing most likely to delay a job by a month. Start it the week you call us.

Timeline

  1. Site visit and written bid: usually within 2 to 4 days of your call.
  2. Disconnects, permit and title: 1 to 3 weeks, mostly waiting on utilities.
  3. Asbestos inspection (older units): 2 to 5 days for results. Add 10 working days if abatement is required.
  4. Demolition and haul-off: 1 day for a single-wide, 1 to 2 days for a double-wide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does mobile home removal cost in Mid-Missouri?

$4,000 to $7,000 for a single-wide and $6,000 to $10,000 for a double-wide. That covers demolition in place, hauling, disposal and a rough graded pad. Decks, add-on rooms, a septic pump and cap, and contents are separate line items on the same bid. Disposal is billed at actual tonnage, $40 to $90 per ton, with the scale tickets attached, and the steel frame scrap is credited back on the invoice. A manufactured home prices the same way.

How much does it cost to demolish a mobile home versus move it?

Demolition is $4,000 to $10,000 and takes one to two days on site. Moving a unit intact typically costs $3,000 to $8,000 for transport plus $2,000 to $5,000 for setup at the new lot, and the home has to be in good enough shape to survive the trip. For anything older than about 2000, in rough condition, or with no lot lined up to receive it, demolition is the cheaper option, and it is over in two days.

What does trailer removal cost if the unit is full of stuff?

Add $500 to $1,500 to the base price. Contents get loaded and hauled with the rest of the debris at the same per-ton rate, and a full single-wide adds 3 to 5 tons. Pull out anything you want to keep before the site visit so the bid is accurate. If the unit was abandoned by a tenant, finish Missouri's notice process to the last known owner and any lienholder before you schedule, because we won't tear down a home somebody else still legally owns.

Do I need a permit to demolish a mobile home in Boone County?

Inside Columbia city limits, yes. The demolition permit comes from City of Columbia Building and Site Development. In unincorporated Boone County there is no county demolition permit, so the requirements are lighter. Either way, every utility has to be disconnected and documented before the excavator arrives, and units built before about 1980 need a Missouri DNR asbestos inspection first. We handle the permit and the disconnect coordination as part of the job, and we tell you what needs your signature.

How long does mobile home removal take?

One day on site for a single-wide and one to two days for a double-wide. The full process, from your first call to a cleared pad, usually runs three to five weeks. Most of that is waiting on utility disconnects, the permit, and title paperwork, not on us. If the utilities are already off and the title is clean, it can be done in under two weeks. Older units add a few days for asbestos lab results.
