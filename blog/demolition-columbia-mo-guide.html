Types of Demolition in Columbia, MO

"Demolition" covers a lot of ground. The type of job decides the equipment, the permit, the timeline, and the price, so it's worth knowing which bucket yours falls in.

  • Full structural teardown. A house, garage, barn, or pole building comes down to the ground, the foundation comes out, and the hole gets backfilled. This is most of what we do on our house, garage and barn demolition jobs.
  • Mobile home removal. Single-wides and double-wides on private land or in a park. The unit gets stripped, the frame gets cut and recycled, and the piers and skirting come out.
  • Interior and selective demolition. A commercial strip-out back to the shell, or taking off one piece of a building (a porch, an addition, a chimney) while the rest stays standing.
  • Concrete demolition. Slabs, footings, sidewalks, driveways, and parking lots. Often part of a teardown, sometimes a job on its own.

If you're not sure which one you've got, that's fine. It gets sorted out on the first site visit. You can see the full list of what we tear down on our demolition services page for Columbia, MO.

Demolition Permits and the Asbestos Rule

Every structural demolition in Columbia, MO needs a permit. Inside city limits it comes from City of Columbia Building and Site Development. Outside the city, Boone County Resource Management issues it. We pull the permit under our name so the scope on the application matches the work we actually do.

The piece that catches people is asbestos. Under the federal NESHAP rule, Missouri requires an asbestos inspection before most structures come down. If the inspector finds regulated material, a licensed abatement contractor has to remove it first. And Missouri DNR requires a demolition notification at least 10 working days before the teardown, even when no asbestos is found. You can read the state's rules on the Missouri DNR asbestos notification page.

Why this matters for your schedule: the 10-working-day clock is two calendar weeks you can't skip. Older Columbia houses, especially ones built before 1980, often have asbestos in siding, floor tile, pipe wrap, or joint compound. We schedule the inspection on day one so it isn't a surprise on demo day.

If you own a dilapidated structure inside the city's Neighborhood Response Team area, it's worth checking the City of Columbia NRT Demolition Program. Qualified owners may get help with demolition costs through federal funds. We walk through the permit process in more detail in our post on demolition permits in Columbia, MO.

Utility Disconnects Come Before the Machine

On any demolition in Columbia, MO, no machine touches a building until every utility is off and confirmed in writing. That means the electric meter pulled, the gas line cut and capped, and water and sewer capped at the property line. In Columbia that usually involves Columbia Water & Light or Boone Electric Cooperative for power, Ameren or Spire for gas depending on where you are, and the city or a sewer district for the sewer cap.

This step causes more delays than anything else, because it depends on utility schedules, not ours. We coordinate all of it and don't start until each one clears. If someone tells you they'll "just work around" a live line, find a different contractor.

Step by Step: How Demolition in Columbia, MO Works

  1. Site visit and written bid. I walk the property, look at the structure, the access, and what's underground, and put a fixed price in writing.
  2. Asbestos inspection. Scheduled right away. If abatement is needed, it happens before anything else.
  3. Permit and DNR notice. We file the demolition permit and the 10-working-day state notification.
  4. Utility disconnects. Electric, gas, water, and sewer, each confirmed before we mobilize.
  5. Demo day. Our 2019 Cat 308 with a breaker, grapple, and thumb handles most residential work. A typical house is on the ground in 1 to 2 days.
  6. Sorting and haul-off. Concrete, steel, and clean wood go to recyclers when it pays. Mixed debris goes to the landfill.
  7. Foundation removal and backfill. Footings and slabs come out. A basement hole gets clean fill, compacted in lifts. We cover this step in detail in our guide to foundation removal and basement fill-in in Columbia, MO.
  8. Rough grade. The disturbed area gets graded so water sheds and a mower can cross it.

From first call to a graded lot, most jobs take 3 to 6 weeks. The teardown itself is the short part. Permits, the asbestos inspection, and utility schedules are where the calendar goes. Our post on how long demolition takes breaks that timeline down week by week.

What Makes Demolition in Mid-Missouri Different

A few local conditions shape demolition in Columbia, MO and the surrounding counties, and national cost guides don't account for any of them.

Clay soil. Mid-Missouri clay holds water. Backfill that gets dumped instead of compacted in lifts will settle, and you'll have a low, soggy spot in a year. It also means we sometimes wait out a wet week, because a loaded dump truck on saturated ground tears up a yard.

Older housing stock. Neighborhoods near downtown Columbia, the Benton-Stephens area, and older parts of Fulton and Boonville have a lot of pre-1980 homes. That means more asbestos inspections that come back positive, more old fuel oil tanks, and more hand-dug basements with stone walls.

Rural lots. Out in the county around Ashland, Hallsville, and Harrisburg, the jobs are often barns, sheds, and old farmhouses. Access is usually easy, but septic tanks, wells, and buried debris piles are common and have to be dealt with the right way.

We work across Columbia, Jefferson City, Fulton, Ashland, Boonville, Moberly, and the rural parts of Boone, Callaway, Cole, Audrain, Howard, Cooper, and Moniteau counties. Permitting is a little different in each jurisdiction, and knowing those differences is part of the job.

How to Pick a Contractor for Demolition in Columbia, MO

Price matters, but the cheapest bid for Columbia, MO demolition is often the most expensive one by the end. Here's what to check before you sign anything:

  • Insurance. Ask for a certificate showing general liability and workers' comp. We carry both, plus an umbrella policy.
  • Who pulls the permit. The contractor should, under their own name.
  • How disposal is billed. A lump sum hides markup. Tonnage at cost with tickets is honest.
  • What "done" means. Get it in writing whether the foundation comes out, whether the basement gets filled, and what grade the lot is left at.
  • Whether they answer the phone. It sounds small. It's the most common complaint we hear about other contractors.

If the lot is going to be built on, ask whether the contractor can take it all the way to a finished pad. We handle backfill, grading, and pad prep through our site work services so you're not hiring a second outfit to finish what the first one started.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to demolish a building in Columbia, MO?

Yes. Any structural demolition inside Columbia city limits needs a permit from City of Columbia Building and Site Development, and outside the city Boone County Resource Management issues it. Small sheds sometimes fall under different rules, but a house, garage, barn, or mobile home always needs one. On top of the local permit, Missouri DNR requires a demolition notification at least 10 working days before most teardowns, along with an asbestos inspection report. A good contractor pulls the permit and files the state notice for you. We handle both on every job so the paperwork matches the actual scope of work.

How much does it cost to tear down a house in Columbia, MO?

Most house teardowns for demolition in Columbia, MO run $8,000 to $22,000 on a slab or crawlspace and $12,000 to $30,000 when there's a basement to fill. The biggest factors are square footage, how much concrete is in the foundation, whether a machine can get close to the house, and what the disposal tonnage turns out to be. Asbestos abatement, if the inspection finds any, is a separate cost paid to a licensed abatement contractor. We price the work as a fixed number for equipment and crew plus disposal billed at the actual scale tickets, so you know exactly where the money goes.

How long does demolition take from start to finish?

For most demolition in Columbia, MO, plan on 3 to 6 weeks from your first call to a graded lot. The teardown itself is fast. A typical house is on the ground in 1 to 2 days, and a garage or shed can come down in a few hours. The rest of the calendar goes to the asbestos inspection, the permit, the 10-working-day DNR notification, and waiting on utility companies to pull the meter and cap the gas. If abatement is needed, add another week or two. Wet weather can also push the backfill and grading, because Mid-Missouri clay won't compact properly when it's soaked.

What happens to the debris after demolition?

We sort it on site. Concrete goes to a recycler, where it gets crushed and reused as base rock. Steel goes to a scrap yard. Clean wood gets separated when it makes sense. Everything else, like drywall, shingles, insulation, and mixed debris, goes to a permitted landfill. Sorting keeps disposal costs down because recyclers charge less per ton than the landfill, and sometimes pay for steel. You get copies of every scale ticket so you can see exactly what left your property and where it went. Personal property left inside the building gets hauled as debris, so clear out anything you want to keep.

What is left on the lot after a demolition?

On our jobs, the building and its foundation come out, any basement hole gets backfilled with clean fill compacted in lifts, and the disturbed area gets a rough grade so water drains away and a mower can cross it. Utility lines are capped at the property line. What's not included unless you ask is topsoil, seed, straw, or a finished building pad. If you're planning to build, we can take the lot to a finished pad with gravel and final grade. If you're just going back to lawn, we can add topsoil and seed. Either way, you get it in writing up front.
