How Much Does Parking Lot Removal Cost in Missouri?

Real per-square-foot ranges for tearing out asphalt and concrete lots, what disposal and base repair add, and what a 40-space lot in Columbia actually runs in 2026.

October 8, 2026 9 min read

If you're trying to figure out parking lot removal cost in Missouri, here's the short answer: asphalt lots run $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot to tear out and haul, and concrete lots run $3 to $6 per square foot. I'm Chris Kurtz, owner of ATLAS Demolition & Site Work in Columbia, MO. We tear out and rebuild lots for churches, strip centers, apartment complexes, and property managers across Mid-Missouri, so the numbers below come from real proposals, not a national calculator.

Quick Answer: Parking lot removal cost in Missouri runs $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot for asphalt and $3 to $6 per square foot for concrete, plus disposal at $40 to $90 per ton. A typical 15,000 square foot lot, about 40 spaces, costs $22,000 to $52,000 to tear out in 2026. Curbs, islands, light pole bases, and a failed base underneath are the things that push a lot toward the top of that range.

Asphalt vs. Concrete Lot Removal: Why the Price Is Different

The biggest single driver of parking lot removal cost is what the lot is made of. Asphalt and concrete come out differently, weigh differently, and go to different places.

Asphalt. Most lots around Columbia are 2 to 4 inches of asphalt over a rock base. Asphalt comes out one of two ways. Full-depth removal means breaking it with the excavator and loading the slabs. Milling means grinding the surface off in a controlled depth with a milling machine, which leaves the base intact and produces millings that recycle easily. Milling is cheaper per foot when the base is sound and you're repaving. Full-depth removal is what you need when the base has failed or the lot is going away entirely.

Concrete. Concrete lots are 5 to 8 inches thick, often reinforced with rebar or wire mesh, and they weigh about 2 tons per 100 square feet at 6 inches. That's roughly double the tonnage of an asphalt lot the same size. The hydraulic breaker on the excavator does the work, the rebar gets cut and separated, and the broken concrete goes to a crusher. More time, more tonnage, more trips. That's why concrete parking lot removal costs about twice what asphalt does.

One more difference. Clean asphalt goes back to the plant or a recycler and becomes new mix or base rock. Clean concrete goes to a crusher and comes back as base rock. Both are cheaper per ton than a landfill, which is why we keep them separated and why mixed loads with dirt, rebar tangles, or trash in them cost you more at the scale.

Parking Lot Removal Cost for a 40-Space Lot, Worked Out

Here's how the numbers stack up on the most common lot we see, a 15,000 square foot asphalt lot with about 40 spaces, a strip of curb along the front, and two islands. This is a typical church, small office, or neighborhood retail lot in Columbia.

  • Asphalt removal, full depth, 15,000 sq ft at $1.50 to $3.50: $22,500 to $52,500
  • Curb and gutter, 200 linear ft at $5 to $12: $1,000 to $2,400
  • Two islands and four light pole bases: $900 to $3,600
  • Disposal, roughly 250 tons of asphalt at $40 to $90: $10,000 to $22,500 (pass-through, included in the removal range above)

Add it up and the parking lot removal cost lands at $24,000 to $58,000 to get that lot down to a clean, graded subgrade. The low end is a 3-inch lot with a sound base and a recycler 15 minutes away. The high end is 4-plus inches of asphalt with a soft base that has to be dug out and hauled as a separate load, and a longer haul to the plant.

If the same lot were concrete instead of asphalt, double the removal line and add about 60 percent to the disposal tonnage. A 15,000 square foot concrete lot runs $45,000 to $90,000 to remove. That sounds like a lot until you compare it to what a 30-year-old concrete lot is costing you in trip-and-fall claims, tenant complaints, and patch jobs that don't hold.

What Moves Parking Lot Removal Cost Up or Down

Two lots with the same square footage can land $20,000 apart. Here's what makes the difference, roughly in order of how much it matters.

  • The base. This is the one that swings the whole job. If the rock base under the asphalt is sound, we take the surface off and leave it. If it's turned to mud, which is common on lots that never had proper drainage, the base has to come out too. That's a second excavation, a second haul as dirty fill, and new rock going back in. A failed base can add $1 to $2 per square foot to parking lot removal cost and far more to replacement. We core or dig a test hole before we quote so you're not surprised.
  • Thickness. Asphalt lots that got overlaid two or three times over the years can be 6 inches thick instead of 3. That's double the tonnage. Concrete at 8 inches with heavy rebar takes longer to break and separate than 5 inches with mesh.
  • Curbs, islands, and what's bolted to the lot. Curb and gutter, planter islands, light pole bases, sign footings, bollards, and dumpster enclosure pads all take their own time. A bare rectangle is cheap. A lot with 12 islands and 8 light poles is not.
  • Haul distance. Columbia and Jefferson City both have asphalt plants and concrete crushers within reasonable range. Lots in smaller towns farther out add trucking time per load, and on a 250-ton job that's real money.
  • Utilities under the lot. Storm inlets, water lines, gas, and electrical conduit to the light poles all have to be located before we break anything. We call in the locate, and if the plans show a storm line 18 inches down where we need to dig the base out, that section gets hand-worked. It's slower and it costs more, but it beats a flooded lot.
  • Phasing and hours. Most owners can't close the lot for two weeks. Splitting the job into phases so half stays open adds mobilization and temporary striping. Night work for a restaurant or weekday-only work for a church adds crew cost. It's usually worth it, but it's on the proposal as its own line.
  • Stormwater and permits. Inside Columbia city limits, disturbing more than an acre or changing the drainage pattern can trigger a land disturbance permit and erosion control. Current requirements are on the City of Columbia permits page. Most removal-only jobs under an acre don't need one, but we check before we start.

Removal Only vs. Removal and Replacement

Most people searching parking lot removal cost are really asking what it costs to get a new lot, and the removal is a third or less of that number. Here's how the two compare on the same 15,000 square foot lot.

  • Removal only: $22,000 to $52,000. The lot comes out, the subgrade gets graded to drain, and you're left with a rock or dirt surface. This is what you buy when the building is coming down too, the lot is being converted to green space, or you've hired a separate paving contractor.
  • Removal plus new asphalt: $60,000 to $120,000. New base rock where needed, 3 inches of hot mix, curbs, and striping. Asphalt opens to traffic in a day or two.
  • Removal plus new concrete: $140,000 to $250,000. New base, 6 inches of reinforced concrete, curbs, and striping. Concrete takes about a week of cure before cars and longer before trucks, and then mostly gets left alone for 30 years.

If you're deciding between the two surfaces, the short version is that asphalt is about half the cost up front and needs sealcoating every few years and a new surface in 15 to 20. Concrete costs more, lasts longer, and is the right call for truck lanes, dumpster pads, and drainage swales even in an asphalt lot. We price both ways on request.

If the lot is coming out as part of a larger teardown, the removal gets bundled with the building demolition and the grading and backfill on one proposal. If you only need a slab or a section of pavement gone rather than a whole lot, our concrete removal service handles smaller jobs at the per-foot ranges in the concrete removal cost guide.

Why Parking Lot Removal Cost Runs Different in Mid-Missouri

National cost sites put asphalt removal at $1 to $2 per square foot. Some of our jobs land there. Most don't, and here's why.

Clay. Boone, Callaway, and Cole counties sit on clay that swells when it's wet and shrinks when it's dry. A lot that was paved straight on clay 25 years ago with a thin layer of rock has a base that's been pumping and failing for a decade. That's the alligator cracking and the low spots that hold water. When we open it up, the base often has to go too, and that's the single biggest reason local parking lot removal cost runs above the national guides.

Freeze-thaw. Mid-Missouri gets 40 to 60 freeze-thaw cycles a winter. Water gets into cracks, freezes, and pries the surface apart. By the time a lot is bad enough to replace, the asphalt is often delaminated into layers that come up in chunks instead of clean slabs, which slows loading, adds tonnage, and pushes parking lot removal cost up.

Timing. Asphalt plants in this part of the state run roughly April through November. If you want the lot removed and repaved in one pass, that's the window. Removal-only work can happen year-round, and some owners tear out in winter and pave in spring to get on the schedule early.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to remove an asphalt parking lot?

Asphalt parking lot removal cost in Missouri runs $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot in 2026, including breaking, loading, and hauling, with disposal billed separately at $40 to $90 per ton. A 15,000 square foot lot, about 40 spaces, comes out at $22,000 to $52,000. The low end is a 3-inch lot on a sound base close to a recycler. The high end is a thick, overlaid lot with a soft base that has to be excavated and hauled as a second load. Curbs, islands, and light pole bases add on top of the per-foot number, and milling instead of full-depth removal can bring the price down when the base is good and you're repaving.

How much does it cost to remove a concrete parking lot?

Concrete parking lot removal runs $3 to $6 per square foot around Columbia, MO, roughly double the cost of asphalt. A 6-inch concrete lot weighs about 2 tons per 100 square feet, so a 15,000 square foot lot is around 300 tons of concrete going to the crusher. Reinforcing steel has to be cut and separated as the slab is broken, which takes more time than loading asphalt chunks. Expect $45,000 to $90,000 to remove a 40-space concrete lot. Clean concrete recycles into base rock at a lower per-ton rate than a landfill, so keeping the loads clean matters to the final disposal number.

Is milling cheaper than full-depth parking lot removal?

Yes, when it's the right fix. Milling grinds off the top 1.5 to 3 inches of asphalt and leaves the base in place, usually at $0.75 to $1.50 per square foot. It only makes sense if the base is sound and you're repaving right away, because the whole point is to reuse what's underneath. If the lot has alligator cracking, soft spots, or standing water, the base has already failed and milling just puts new asphalt on the same problem. We core or dig a test hole during the walk-through, and the proposal says which approach we're recommending and why.

Can the parking lot stay open during removal?

Almost always, if we plan for it. We split the lot into two or three phases, keep the entrance and the front rows open, and put down temporary striping so the open section functions. Churches get cleared out and reopened for Sunday. Restaurants and retail can be done overnight. Apartment lots get phased by building so nobody loses their spot for more than a day or two. Phasing adds some mobilization cost, but it's a line item on the proposal, and for most commercial owners it's cheaper than closing for two weeks.

What happens to the old asphalt and concrete?

Both get recycled. Asphalt goes back to the plant or an aggregate recycler as millings or broken slab and becomes new hot mix or base material. Concrete goes to a crusher and comes back as base rock, sometimes under the same lot it came out of. Recycling is cheaper per ton than a landfill, which is why we keep asphalt, concrete, and dirt in separate loads. Every load crosses a scale, and the tickets are attached to your invoice so the disposal line is exactly what we paid, with no markup.
