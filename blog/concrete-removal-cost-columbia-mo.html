Concrete Removal Cost Per Square Foot

Per-square-foot is the right way to think about flatwork. Foundation walls and footings price by the linear foot, because depth and reinforcement vary too much to put on a square-foot basis. Everything in the table is a typical all-in concrete removal cost per square foot for Columbia and the surrounding counties in 2026, disposal included. A site visit turns it into a firm number.

2026 rates by slab type

Slab type Low High Notes
4-inch unreinforced patio, walk or shed pad $3.00 / sq ft $5.00 / sq ft Breaks with the bucket
4-inch driveway with wire mesh $3.50 / sq ft $5.50 / sq ft Mesh pulled and cut as it comes up
6-inch reinforced driveway or garage slab $5.00 / sq ft $8.00 / sq ft Breaker on every foot; rebar sheared
6- to 8-inch commercial lot or loading apron $3.00 / sq ft $8.00 / sq ft Big lots $3 to $6 on volume; small thick aprons at the high end

How the bid is structured: mobilization, the excavator, the skid steer and the crew are a fixed price. Disposal is a pass-through at actual loads and tonnage, scale tickets attached. Clean concrete going to a recycler sits at the low end of that $40 to $90 range; concrete mixed with dirt, asphalt or rebar goes to the landfill at the high end.

For sizing the disposal line: 4-inch concrete weighs about 50 pounds per square foot, 6-inch about 75. A 600-square-foot driveway at 4 inches is 15 tons, two or three truckloads, $600 to $1,350 in disposal before anyone touches a breaker.

Worked Examples

Three jobs we price all the time, with the math shown.

600 sq ft driveway, 4-inch, wire mesh, straight shot from the street

The most common residential removal. Two-car driveway, 20 by 30 feet, cracked and heaved. The Cat 308 with the breaker busts it into loadable pieces, the skid steer loads the roll-off, the mesh gets pulled and cut as we go. About 15 tons, one day on site.

  • Removal: 600 sq ft x $3.50 to $5.50 = $2,100 to $3,300
  • Of that, disposal at 15 tons x $40 to $90 = $600 to $1,350, billed at actual
  • If the driveway is 6-inch with rebar (common on newer builds and anything a truck parks on): 600 x $5 to $8 = $3,000 to $4,800, about 22 tons

1,200 sq ft patio and walkway, backyard, through a fence gate

A big 4-inch unreinforced patio behind the house plus a side walk. Access is the whole story. Pull two fence panels and drive the 308 in, and it is a one-day job. A 36-inch gate and a slope means a stand-up mini skid steer and 20 to 30 percent more.

  • Removal: 1,200 sq ft x $3 to $5 = $3,600 to $6,000; most land at $4,200 to $5,500 with decent access
  • Disposal: 30 tons x $40 to $90 = $1,200 to $2,700
  • Saw-cut where the walk meets the front stoop you are keeping: a few hundred dollars, priced by the linear foot

12,000 sq ft parking lot, commercial

A 40-space lot for a church, a retail strip or a shop building. Asphalt comes up in sheets with the excavator bucket. Six-inch concrete with rebar has to be broken and cut, piece by piece. Three to six days on site with a 13- to 15-ton excavator rented for the job.

  • Asphalt, 3 inches: 12,000 x $1.50 to $3.50 = $18,000 to $42,000; roughly 215 tons of millings
  • Concrete, 6 inches reinforced: 12,000 x $3 to $6 = $36,000 to $72,000; roughly 450 tons
  • Curbs, islands and light-pole bases are line items on top

When Per-Square-Foot Pricing Misleads

A concrete removal cost per square foot is a planning number. Multiply it blindly and you will be wrong in both directions. Five places it breaks.

Job minimums on small pads

A 100-square-foot stoop does not cost $500. The trailer trip, the utility locate and the dump run cost the same whether we break 100 square feet or 1,000, so small pads carry a mobilization minimum of $600 to $1,000. Under about 300 square feet the minimum is the price and the per-foot rate is irrelevant, which is why we bundle a stoop or short walk with a driveway whenever we can.

Access surcharges

The table assumes a machine can reach the slab. A backyard pad through a 36-inch gate, a basement floor in a house that is staying, or a slab 150 feet from where the roll-off can sit means a smaller machine and double handling. Figure 20 to 40 percent on top, and double on a hand-carry job.

Saw-cutting is billed per linear foot

Anywhere the removal meets concrete you are keeping (garage floor at the apron, city sidewalk at the driveway) we saw-cut a straight line first so the new pour has a clean edge to butt to. It is billed per linear foot, not per square foot: typically $4 to $8 per linear foot on 4-inch flatwork, more for full-depth cuts through reinforced slab. A 600-square-foot driveway with 40 feet of cut adds $160 to $320.

Hauling and dump fees are pass-through

Disposal is not our margin. You pay what the recycler or landfill charges at actual tonnage, scale tickets attached. Two slabs of identical square footage can differ by $500 or more on whether the load is clean enough for the recycler or goes to the landfill with dirt and roots in it.

Why two 600 sq ft driveways can differ by 40 percent

Driveway one is 4-inch with mesh, straight shot from the street, clean concrete to the recycler at $40 a ton: about $2,400 with a short saw-cut. Driveway two is 6-inch with rebar on 12-inch centers, a retaining wall on one side so the truck sits in the street, and enough dirt in the load to send it to the landfill at $90 a ton: closer to $3,400. Same square footage, 40 percent apart, and both are a correct concrete removal cost per square foot for that slab.

Volume cuts the other way. A 12,000-square-foot lot at 6 inches prices at $3 to $6 while a 600-square-foot 6-inch driveway prices at $5 to $8, because setup is spread over twenty times the area and a bigger excavator breaks faster per foot.

What Drives the Price

Thickness

Four inches breaks with the bucket and a breaker. Six inches needs the breaker on every square foot. Eight inches and up, common on old commercial slabs and loading docks, takes twice the time per square foot of a patio. Thickness also drives tonnage, and tonnage drives disposal.

Rebar and wire mesh

Wire mesh holds the pieces together so they have to be pulled apart and cut. Rebar on 12- or 18-inch centers is real work: every bar gets cut with the shear or a torch before the piece can be loaded, and a recycler will reject loads with too much steel. Reinforced concrete is $2 to $3 per square foot more than unreinforced.

Distance to the recycler

Clean concrete goes to a recycler on the edge of town that crushes it into base rock. From Fulton, Boonville or Moberly the round trip is 45 to 90 minutes per load. A few hundred dollars on a driveway, a few thousand on a parking lot.

Asphalt Removal Cost vs. Concrete Removal Cost

Asphalt is cheaper to remove than concrete, usually $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot against $3 to $8. Three reasons. It is thinner: 2 to 4 inches over rock, not 4 to 8 inches of concrete. It has no steel in it. And it does not have to be broken: a milling machine grinds it off in one pass, or the excavator bucket peels it up in sheets, and the millings go to an asphalt plant or back down as base. It hauls lighter too, about 36 pounds per square foot for 3-inch asphalt against 50 for 4-inch concrete.

We remove and replace asphalt as well as concrete, and on a parking lot what goes back is a bigger decision than what comes out. Our asphalt vs. concrete parking lot comparison covers up-front cost, lifespan and what Mid-Missouri clay and freeze-thaw do to each surface. The exception is an old lot with several overlays stacked up, 6 or 8 inches of asphalt built over decades. That mills slow, hauls heavy and prices like concrete.

Why We Don't Do Leveling or Mudjacking

People ask if we can lift a sunken slab instead of replacing it. We do not do concrete leveling, mudjacking, foam injection or patch repair, on purpose. Leveling fixes a symptom. The slab sank because the base under it failed, and pumping material under it does not fix the base. A lot of leveled slabs are back on the ground in three to five years.

When a slab is heaved, cracked through, or sunk more than an inch or two, our answer is tear it out, fix the base, pour it right. It costs more up front and it is done for 25 to 30 years. If the slab is sound and just needs a cosmetic lift, a leveling company is the right call and we will tell you so.

Recycling and Disposal

Missouri DNR treats clean, uncontaminated concrete as clean fill, not solid waste. It can be crushed into base rock, placed as fill on an approved site, or landfilled. We send it to a recycler whenever the load is clean enough, which is most residential flatwork, and the lower gate rate shows up on your disposal line because it is billed at actual cost. Loads heavy in rebar, dirt and roots, or paint and sealer go to the landfill. On a parking lot we separate asphalt from concrete as we go.

How to Get an Accurate Per-Square-Foot Number

Five minutes with a tape gets you a real concrete removal cost per square foot instead of a guess. Send us:

  1. Length times width. Pace it or tape it. 20 by 30 is 600 square feet; break odd shapes into rectangles.
  2. Thickness at an edge or a core. Measure the exposed edge where the slab meets grass or a step. No exposed edge, a crack you can get a tape into or a 1-inch core works. If you have to guess: 4 inches for patios and walks, 5 to 6 for driveways and garage floors.
  3. Rebar or mesh visible at cracks. Look into any crack or spalled corner. Rusty wire is mesh. A half-inch bar is rebar. Steel moves the rate $2 to $3.
  4. Access width. The narrowest gate or side yard a machine has to pass, and how far the slab sits from where a truck can park.
  5. Photos. Four or five, including the approach from the street.

Text them to (573) 234-6641 or attach them to the consultation form. We give you a working range from the photos and confirm it on a site visit, where we probe a crack for steel, check thickness and walk the access route. Permits

Standalone concrete removal in Columbia, inside or outside city limits, does not need a demolition permit. Two exceptions: concrete that is part of a structure coming down is covered by the demolition permit from City of Columbia Building and Site Development (inside city limits) or Boone County Resource Management (outside), and a new driveway approach or sidewalk in the city right-of-way needs its own permit. We handle both. See our demolition permit guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does concrete removal cost per square foot in Columbia, MO?

$3 to $5 per square foot for 4-inch unreinforced concrete, $3.50 to $5.50 for a 4-inch driveway with wire mesh, and $5 to $8 for 6-inch reinforced, including breaking, loading, hauling and disposal. Large commercial lots price at $3 to $6 on volume, and asphalt removal is $1.50 to $3.50. Disposal is billed as a pass-through at $40 to $90 per ton.

How much does it cost to remove a concrete driveway?

A typical 600-square-foot two-car driveway is $2,100 to $3,300 to remove if it is 4-inch with mesh, and $3,000 to $4,800 if it is 6-inch with rebar. That is about 15 to 22 tons of concrete and one day on site. Long rural driveways and ones with poor access price higher.

How much does it cost to remove and replace a concrete driveway?

Removal plus a new 4-inch reinforced pour on a 600-square-foot driveway runs $6,900 to $11,700, using $8 to $14 per square foot for new concrete. Replacing with asphalt instead is $4,500 to $8,100. We do both under one contract; the pour is done by our concrete crew under our warranty.

How much does parking lot removal cost?

Asphalt lots run $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot and concrete lots $3 to $6, so a 12,000-square-foot lot is $18,000 to $42,000 in asphalt or $36,000 to $72,000 in concrete. Curbs, islands and pole bases are extra. Plan on three to six days on site.

Can you level or mudjack my slab instead of removing it?

No. We do not do concrete leveling, mudjacking, foam injection or patch repair. Leveling does not fix the failed base that caused the slab to sink, and lifted slabs often crack or settle again within a few years. If a slab is heaved, cracked through or sunk more than an inch or two, we remove it, fix the base and pour it right.

How long does concrete removal take?

Patios and walks take half a day to a day. A driveway is one day. Foundation slabs take one to two days, parking lots three to six. From your call to a finished job is usually one to two weeks, most of it waiting on the utility locate and scheduling.

Do I need a permit to remove concrete in Columbia, MO?

Not for a standalone driveway, patio or sidewalk removal. If the concrete is part of a building demolition, the demolition permit from the city's Building and Site Development office or Boone County Resource Management covers it. New work in the city right-of-way, like a driveway approach, needs its own permit. We handle both.

What is the cheapest way to remove a concrete slab?

For a small slab, under about 100 square feet, 4 inches thick and no steel, a homeowner with a rented electric breaker and a trailer can break it and run it to the recycler for the rental and the gate fee. Above a few hundred square feet, a contractor with a hydraulic breaker and a dump truck is the cheapest per square foot: the machine breaks in an hour what takes a weekend by hand, and one truck moves 15 tons in two trips. Renting a breaker and paying dump fees yourself rarely saves much once you count the rental, the trailer loads and your time.
